When to get help immediately

  • Sudden hearing loss in one ear – see an ENT doctor within 1–2 days; early treatment can save hearing
  • Difficulty breathing, noisy breathing or inability to swallow saliva
  • A nosebleed that does not stop after 20 minutes of firm pressure
  • Swelling of the face or neck with fever and pain

Know the signs

Care for your ears, nose and throat.

Ear care

Do
  • Clean only the outer ear with a soft cloth
  • Keep ears dry after bathing or swimming
Don't
  • Put cotton buds, hairpins or keys inside the ear
  • Pour oil or home remedies into a painful or discharging ear

Protect your hearing

  • Keep earphone volume below 60% and take breaks every hour
  • Use ear protection near loud machines and fireworks
  • Get a hearing check if you often ask people to repeat, or turn up the TV
  • Ringing in the ears (tinnitus) that doesn't go away needs an ENT check

Children's ears

  • Ear pain, pulling at the ear or fever with a cold can mean an ear infection
  • Do not feed babies while they lie flat
  • Watch for delayed speech or not responding to sounds – hearing should be checked early

Sinus & nose

See an ENT doctor if you have
  • Blocked nose, facial pain or thick discharge for more than 10 days
  • Blockage on one side only, or repeated nosebleeds
  • Loss of smell

Avoid using decongestant nose sprays for more than 3–5 days without advice – they can worsen the blockage.

Throat & voice

  • Drink enough water and avoid shouting or whispering for long periods
  • Smoking, tobacco and alcohol damage the throat and voice box
  • Acidity can irritate the throat – avoid late, heavy meals
Don't ignore
  • Hoarseness lasting more than 3 weeks
  • Difficulty swallowing, or a lump in the neck

Snoring

Loud snoring with pauses in breathing, choking at night or daytime sleepiness may be sleep apnoea. It can affect the heart and blood pressure and should be evaluated.

Myths & facts

Let's clear up some myths.

"Oil in the ear cleans it and cures ear pain."

Fact: Ears clean themselves. Oil or drops in an infected ear or a perforated eardrum can make things worse. See an ENT doctor for ear pain or discharge.

"A cold or sore throat always needs antibiotics."

Fact: Most colds and sore throats are caused by viruses and get better without antibiotics. Unnecessary antibiotics cause side effects and resistance – take them only when a doctor prescribes.

"Hearing loss is just old age – nothing can be done."

Fact: Many causes of hearing loss, like wax, infections or fluid behind the eardrum, are treatable. For permanent hearing loss, hearing aids can greatly improve daily life.
