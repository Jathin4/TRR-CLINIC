Know the signs

Your mind needs care too.

Depression

Signs to watch for (2 weeks or more)
  • Low mood or loss of interest in daily activities
  • Poor sleep or sleeping too much; low energy
  • Changes in appetite or weight
  • Feeling worthless, guilty or hopeless
What helps

Talking to a psychiatrist early. Counselling, medicines when needed, regular sleep, daily activity and staying connected with family.

Anxiety & panic

Signs to watch for
  • Constant worry that is hard to control
  • Racing heart, sweating, trembling, breathlessness
  • Sudden attacks of intense fear
  • Avoiding places or people because of fear
What helps

Slow breathing, limiting tea/coffee, regular exercise, and therapy or medicines advised by your doctor.

Sleep problems

Healthy sleep habits
  • Sleep and wake at the same time every day
  • No phone or TV for 30–60 minutes before bed
  • Avoid tea, coffee and heavy meals in the evening
  • Do not take sleeping pills without a prescription

If poor sleep lasts more than a few weeks, it may be a sign of stress, depression or another condition – consult a doctor.

Alcohol & addiction

Warning signs
  • Needing more to get the same effect
  • Shaking, sweating or restlessness when you stop
  • Problems at home or work because of drinking or tobacco use
Important

Do not stop heavy, long-term drinking suddenly at home – withdrawal can be dangerous. De-addiction treatment is safe and confidential with medical support.

Supporting a family member

  • Listen calmly without judging or blaming
  • Encourage them to see a doctor – offer to go with them
  • Help them take medicines regularly and attend follow-ups
  • Take care of your own health and rest too

When to see a psychiatrist

  • Feelings or behaviour that affect work, studies or relationships
  • Stress that does not improve with rest and support
  • Memory problems or confusion in elderly family members
  • Children with major changes in behaviour, mood or school performance
Medicine safety

Use medicines the right way.

Take them as prescribed

  • Follow the dose and timing on your prescription
  • Do not stop or change medicines on your own, even if you feel better
  • Many mental health medicines take 2–6 weeks to show full benefit – be patient

Be safe

  • Never share your medicines or take someone else's
  • Avoid alcohol with psychiatric or sleep medicines
  • Tell your doctor about all other medicines you take, and if you are pregnant or breastfeeding

Store them well

  • Keep in a cool, dry place, away from sunlight
  • Keep out of reach of children
  • Bring your old prescriptions and medicine strips to every visit
Myths & facts

Let's clear up some myths.

"Seeing a psychiatrist means you are 'mad'."

Fact: Psychiatrists treat common problems like stress, sleep issues, anxiety and depression. Seeking help is a sign of strength, and consultations are confidential.

"Psychiatric medicines are addictive and must be taken for life."

Fact: Most commonly used medicines are not habit-forming when taken as prescribed. Many people take them for a limited period; your doctor will guide how and when to reduce them safely.

"Depression is just weakness – they should just think positive."

Fact: Depression is a medical condition involving the brain. It is not a choice or a character flaw, and it responds well to treatment.
